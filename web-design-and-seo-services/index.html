Estimate the scope↓

01

One team when the decisions depend on each other.

An integrated project makes sense when your website is new, its structure no longer reflects the business, or a redesign must preserve existing search visibility. In these situations, design decisions affect content, URL choices affect migration, and both affect what can be measured after launch. A shared brief reduces the need to reconstruct those decisions between suppliers.

It is not the right answer to every task. If your structure, content and search plan are already sound, development alone may be enough. If the website works well but organic visibility needs attention, a separate SEO engagement may be more appropriate. We define the gap before recommending the broader scope.

02

Research before the full page list is fixed.

We review your offer, audiences, competitors and search demand alongside the existing website and available business evidence. Commercial queries help identify the services people are looking for and the information needed to choose a provider. The research also considers what your team can deliver and what distinguishes the offer beyond a generic list of features.

This produces a proposed structure, priorities and content requirements. An initial estimate is not permission to expand the project indefinitely: if research changes the scope, the revised work and price must be agreed before implementation. Search volume guides prioritisation; it does not justify a traffic or revenue promise.

03

Map each intent to a useful destination.

The architecture assigns related query groups to clear URLs and separates service pages, industries, cases and supporting information. Some questions belong in a section rather than on a new page. Others need a dedicated landing page because the audience, offer and decision are genuinely different. The aim is to make the site easier to use without manufacturing thin pages.

We define navigation and internal links with the page map, not as finishing touches. Existing URLs and their performance inform the plan. Content owners can see what must be written, what can be reused and which evidence is missing before design depends on it. Language versions receive their own real equivalents rather than automatic empty copies.

04

Design and development follow the enquiry journey.

Design brings the offer, proof and next action into a readable hierarchy. We consider long service names, mobile content, form fields and real case material before treating the visual direction as complete. The website should make it clear who the service is for, what is included and how to enquire without hiding essential information inside an animation.

Development implements the agreed layouts and interactions, with responsive behaviour and content access tested across screen sizes. Forms, navigation and links are functional parts of the product, not decorative placeholders. Integrations and unusual application logic are estimated separately; a standard website scope should not silently promise a bespoke software platform.

05

Prepare search access and measurement before publication.

Before launch, we check indexability, canonical URLs, metadata, internal links, sitemap entries and structured data against the actual pages. Important content must be available to search engines and visitors. Performance checks consider the page people will use, including images, fonts and interactive elements. Passing a tool’s checklist alone is not evidence that a page answers its commercial intent.

The measurement plan names the actions that matter: an enquiry submission, a call interaction or another agreed step. Consent controls determine when analytics may run. We separate successful submissions from simple button clicks, avoid duplicate events and keep personal or sensitive form content out of analytics. The site must still work when a visitor declines optional tracking.

06

Treat a redesign as a controlled migration.

For an existing website, we inventory URLs, valuable content and known search signals before deciding what changes. The migration plan maps replaced URLs to relevant destinations and identifies pages that should remain. Forms, integrations and redirects are checked before release, with access to the previous version retained for recovery where the hosting arrangement permits it.

After publication, we check response codes, crawling and measurement again. Preserving a URL alone cannot guarantee preserved rankings, but avoidable broken links and missing content should not be accepted as the cost of a new design. Complex CMS migrations and legacy systems require diagnosis and a separately agreed implementation scope.

07

A one-off project, followed by a separate SEO programme.

Research, design, development and the agreed launch work form the website project. Ongoing SEO starts from the published site and has its own priorities, scope and monthly estimate. The calculator below shows these amounts separately. The proposal makes clear which work belongs to the launch and which requires a recurring engagement.

If you choose the existing commercial-launch option, it includes the defined initial advertising and analytics setup as well as baseline SEO. That does not turn paid campaigns into organic SEO or include future media spend. We clarify the chosen option, exclusions and responsibilities in the proposal so you can compare it with development-only or SEO-only work.

08

Use the first 90 days to establish evidence.

The first 30 days focus on release checks, indexation, working measurement and correcting issues found on the live site. Around days 31–60, the review can use emerging queries and landing-page evidence to prioritise content and internal-link improvements. Around days 61–90, we assess what has changed and plan the next cycle instead of mechanically publishing more pages.

This is a planning framework, not a guaranteed schedule of search results. Data may be limited for a new domain, and approvals or technical dependencies can change the order. We document those limits and distinguish work completed from outcomes observed. The next decision should be based on useful evidence, not a promise that every website matures in three months.

FAQ

Before you decide

Can I order only the website or only SEO?+

Yes. The integrated approach is useful when the decisions overlap, but it does not make every service mandatory. We can discuss development-only work or ongoing SEO separately after reviewing your starting point.

Is ongoing SEO included in the launch price?+

No. The one-off website or commercial-launch estimate and the monthly SEO estimate are separate. The proposal defines when each engagement starts, its scope and any work that would be quoted additionally.

Will a redesign keep all our rankings?+

No supplier can guarantee that. We plan the migration around existing URLs, content and evidence, check redirects and monitor the release. Search engines and competitors can still change the results.

What do you need for an initial discussion?+

Your current website, main services, target market and the business change you want to achieve. If you have Search Console or analytics evidence, we can discuss appropriate access after the initial enquiry. Do not send passwords through the form.
