01

For practices, clinics and dental groups.

Dental SEO suits an independent practice, a clinic or a network with defined treatments, clinicians and locations. We start with what you can genuinely offer: where patients travel from, which services have capacity, and which enquiries the team can handle. A broad keyword list is not useful if it promotes unavailable treatments or locations you do not serve.

An existing website may need focused improvements rather than replacement. If you are creating a new site, search research can inform its structure before development. The distinction matters: building the website is a project; improving its organic visibility is an ongoing programme with a separate scope.

02

Research the decision behind the query.

Patients may search by treatment, concern, clinician or place. We group commercial queries by intent and inspect the pages already appearing in search. Treatment demand, a question about symptoms and a search for opening hours need different answers. Informational queries can support the journey, but they should not displace pages that explain your actual services.

The output is a prioritised query-to-page map, with existing URLs to improve and genuine gaps to fill. We consider local competition, your current search visibility and the material available from the practice. Search volume helps compare opportunities; it is not a forecast of visits, appointments or revenue.

03

Give each page a clear job.

Treatment pages explain the service, who provides it, the consultation process and the next step. Clinician profiles establish relevant qualifications and experience. Technology pages describe equipment in context, without treating a brand name as proof of a clinical outcome. Where appropriate, price information explains what a quoted amount includes and what requires an individual assessment.

Location pages are reserved for real premises or a genuinely distinct service context. Internal links connect treatments, clinicians and practical information so visitors can make an informed enquiry. We avoid multiplying near-identical pages for every nearby town or making several pages compete for the same treatment query.

04

Remove technical obstacles to useful pages.

We check whether important pages can be crawled and indexed, how duplicate URLs are handled, and whether canonical tags, sitemap entries and internal links agree. The review also covers page loading, mobile usability and structured data that matches the visible content. Technical fixes are prioritised by their effect on access and the patient journey, not the number of warnings in a tool.

For a redesign or CMS change, the work includes an inventory of existing URLs, a redirect plan, checks before launch and follow-up monitoring. A migration needs its own agreed scope. Keeping valuable content and working links is usually more useful than replacing every URL simply to make the site look new.

05

Make local presence and medical content consistent.

Business profiles should reflect real practice details: name, address, contact information, opening hours and services. We look for inconsistencies between those profiles and the website, and identify practical improvements the practice can maintain. Reviews and photographs must be authentic and used with the necessary permission. We do not invent local offices, patient stories or clinical credentials.

Content is written in accessible language and reviewed by the practice’s qualified specialists for medical accuracy. Legal review remains with an appropriate adviser for the target market. Pages should explain a consultation clearly without guaranteeing a treatment result. SEO work does not replace a clinician’s judgement or authorise the publication of confidential patient information.

06

Measure enquiries as well as visibility.

Search Console, query monitoring and landing-page data help show where visibility changes. Forms, phone calls and messenger actions help connect visits to enquiries where measurement is permitted and technically available. These are different signals: a contact-button click is not a confirmed appointment, and a ranking improvement does not prove that SEO caused a change in bookings.

We agree which actions matter and what your team can report about enquiry quality. Reporting separates branded and non-branded demand where the available data supports it, notes changes to campaigns or the website, and records limitations. Patient details and the content of medical conversations should not be sent to analytics platforms.

07

A monthly cycle with visible priorities.

Each cycle starts by reviewing search performance, technical issues and the previous changes. We choose the next priorities, update the relevant pages or technical settings, and record what was changed. The next review checks whether those changes were indexed and whether the available evidence supports keeping, adjusting or expanding the work.

The working plan depends on access to the website, approvals and clinical input. It is not a promise to produce a fixed number of articles regardless of need. We agree responsibilities before starting, including who approves medical statements and who handles incoming enquiries. Neither search positions nor a number of leads is guaranteed.

FAQ

Before you decide

Do we need a new website before starting dental SEO?+

Not necessarily. We first check the existing structure, content, technical restrictions and access. If the site can support the required improvements, replacing it may be unnecessary. A rebuild is proposed only when the limitations justify a separate development project.

Can you guarantee first-page rankings or appointments?+

No. Search results, competitors and patient decisions are outside any agency’s control. We agree the work, measurement and review process, not a guaranteed position, treatment outcome or volume of appointments.

Does the monthly estimate include redesign and advertising?+

No. Ongoing SEO is estimated separately from website development, migration and paid campaigns. The proposal identifies the actual pages, queries and technical work, and any separate services needed.

Who checks treatment information?+

The practice’s qualified clinicians approve medical content. Appropriate specialists review legal requirements. We organise, write and implement the content within that review process; we do not act as the clinical or legal authority.
